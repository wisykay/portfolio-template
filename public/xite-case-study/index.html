The Solution

From shadow sessions to a validated React prototype in 4 weeks. Here's how the research shaped every design decision.

The Constraints That Shaped Everything

01

420-second warm-up

The AI scoring engine needs 7 minutes of buffered signal data before it can produce a single result. You can't show a progress bar for something that hasn't started yet.

Phase-based progress — "Buffering signals" → "Scoring sleep stages" → "Generating outputs" — so users understand why it's waiting, not just that it's waiting.

02

"Black box" anxiety

The AI team wanted full automation. Clinicians wanted full control. This wasn't an edge case — it was the core tension of the project.

AI proposes with confidence scores. Clinician confirms. Every mapping is visible, every override is logged. Trust through transparency.

03

Channel naming chaos

Files arrive from global sleep labs with inconsistent names. The same electrode, three different labels — EMG Mental, EEG Fp1-A2, SpO2_Pulse — all needing standardization.

AI auto-maps with confidence scores. Ambiguous channels get flagged, not guessed. Clinicians confirm once, save as a Site Profile for future batches.

Key Design Decisions

How do you prevent errors in a 40-minute manual process?

You don't let them start. A sequential wizard where each step validates before unlocking the next. No skipping. No guessing. The system catches what humans miss.

Upload→ Setup→ Map→ Lights→ Validate→ Process

How do you automate channel mapping without removing clinical control?

Worked with the Philips AI team to embed AASM compliance rules directly into the mapping step. The system auto-detects and validates — but the clinician confirms. Trust through transparency, not black-box automation.

How do you prove it works before engineering builds it?

Functional React prototype. 3 sleep technologists. Full end-to-end study processing. Zero-assist task completion — no one asked for help, no one got stuck.

What I Learned

01

Constraints sharpen design

I cut 3 features that felt essential. But shipping a validated wizard that solves the core problem beats shipping a feature-rich tool that's 60% done.

02

Users don't want faster — they want safer

Every brief said "reduce time." Shadow sessions revealed they were slow because they were triple-checking. The reframe to "make it trustworthy" changed everything.

03

Designing with AI = designing the boundaries

AI team wanted full automation. Clinicians wanted full control. The solution: show the AI's work transparently so the clinician can verify and confirm.

If I had 8 more weeks
Batch processing / Custom templates / Scoring preview

Features scoped, validated with users, and ready to build — queued for the next sprint if this project continues.
